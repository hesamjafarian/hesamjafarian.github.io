This project explores a bridge-centered monitoring concept for protecting critical maritime infrastructure and improving situational awareness around sensitive waterways. The approach combines a simulated maritime environment with a camera-array design positioned to observe bridge surroundings, vessel approaches, support structures, and protected zones.

The monitoring concept uses multiple overlapping camera views to reduce blind spots and improve visibility across the area of interest. By observing vessels from different viewpoints, the system can support more reliable detection, tracking, and activity monitoring around the infrastructure.

The simulation environment is used to evaluate camera placement, field of view, coverage overlap, and monitoring effectiveness before real-world deployment. This makes it possible to study different vessel trajectories, traffic conditions, and surveillance scenarios in a controlled and repeatable setting.

The framework is intended to support:

- wide-area surveillance around bridges and critical maritime assets
- multi-view vessel detection and tracking
- monitoring of vessel approaches and restricted areas
- reduction of camera blind spots through overlapping coverage
- detection of unusual movement, loitering, or unexpected vessel behavior
- evaluation of camera layouts and surveillance strategies in simulation

The project demonstrates how simulation and sensor-layout modelling can be used together to design more effective monitoring systems for critical infrastructure, while providing a foundation for future multi-sensor surveillance and security applications.
